# Badinerie in Motion

A scrolling piano roll for J. S. Bach's Orchestral Suite No. 2 in B minor, BWV 1067: all seven movements, from the Ouverture to the *Badinerie*. Watch the notes go by, hover any note to hear it, and drag or scroll to move through the piece.

It's a static site: `index.html` plus the MIDI files in `midi/`, with no build step. Serve it with any static host (GitHub Pages works), or locally with `npm run serve` or `python3 -m http.server`. Opening `index.html` straight from disk (`file://`) won't load the movements, because browsers block `fetch` there.

## The notes

The app ships with all seven movements, as MIDI files in `midi/`, from John Hamm's 1995 General MIDI sequence of the Orchestral Suites: Ouverture, Rondeau, Sarabande, Bourrée I & II, Polonaise & Double, Menuet and Badinerie. Each has five parts: flute, violin I, violin II, viola and cello (continuo). All repeats are played, and Hamm added a few ornaments of his own. Pick a movement from the tabs, or link to one directly with `#ouverture`, `#rondeau`, `#sarabande`, `#bourree`, `#polonaise`, `#menuet` or `#badinerie` (the fugue below has its own `#fugue`).

There's also a tab for the **Great Fugue** from the Fantasia and Fugue in G minor, BWV 542 (`midi/fugue.mid`, link `#fugue`). It comes from the [Mutopia Project](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/cgibin/piece-info.cgi?id=984)'s LilyPond engraving of the organ piece, so its voices are the two hands and the pedal, not the fugue's four voices. The source file also had octave-coupler tracks (the same notes again an octave up or down), which were left out. By default it plays on the app's bowed-string sound; pick **Organ** under **Sound** to hear it on something closer to the real thing. Only the Fugue is included, not the Fantasia.

Playback follows each file's tempo map (the Ouverture's fast section, the closing ritardandos) and time-signature changes. The speed slider scales all of it.

Each voice gets its own colour and its own sound (a breathy flute, or a bowed string). The **Sound** menu (under **More**) swaps that for one instrument across all voices instead: Organ, Harpsichord, Piano, Lute, Electric piano, Marimba, Vibraphone, Music box, Clarinet, Oboe, Brass, Choir, Chiptune or Synth pad. All of them are synthesised in the browser, with no samples. **Random mix** deals every voice an instrument of its own, no two alike, and lists who got what under the menu; **Shuffle** deals again. The choice is remembered on this device (a random mix is dealt afresh for each piece). Click a voice chip to hide and mute it, or the ◎ next to it to put that voice in the spotlight: the others keep playing at about a third of the level and fade back in the picture.

You can load any other MIDI file with **Load MIDI…** (under **More**) or by dragging it onto the lane. Each track becomes a voice. Tracks that share a name and channel are merged, because some files split the repeats across separate tracks.

MIDI files rarely mark an upbeat. If the first note isn't on a barline, the app treats that bar as the pickup. Otherwise, if the piece ends on a short bar, it treats the missing part as the pickup. Either way, bar 1 of the Badinerie starts on the F♯ after the quarter-note pickup.

## Controls

- Hover a note to hear it, or click/tap it
- **Hold chord** (while paused): every note under the line sustains until the line leaves it, so you can hear the harmony. The readout names the chord (`Bm`, `F♯7`, `D/F♯`…) with [Tonal](https://github.com/tonaljs/tonal), preferring common triads and seventh chords over exotic readings; offline it shows just the notes. Drag the line itself, or drag the score under it. `←` `→` step from chord to chord, `Shift` + `←` `→` jump a bar. **Arpeggio** (or `A`) toggles a running arpeggio of the held chord, up and down, that follows the line; **Arp** sets its speed (2–16 notes per second)
- **Sing** (needs a microphone and headphones: the browser's echo cancellation is switched off, because it fades out held notes): turn it on, pick your **Voice** next to it, and the music plays on without that voice, starting a bar before its next note. Sing along in any octave: your pitch is drawn over the score, and notes you hit in time get a gold bar. Miss a note and the music stops on it, holds the harmony and plays the note for you, then carries on once you sing it (`Space` skips it). Notes too quick to sing at the current speed never stop it, so lower **Speed** to be asked for more. Where your voice has several notes at once (a chord in one of the Fugue's hands, say), sing them all, one after another from the lowest up: the lowest is due in time like any other note, then the music stops on the chord and holds it until you've sung the rest (the tag counts them, `Sing 2/3`), and the chord counts as one note. **Guide** plays your voice softly along with you. The tag counts notes sung in time (✓), notes the music waited for, and your streak of notes in a row. `H` plays the next note. Pitch comes from the microphone with the YIN method, and nothing is recorded or sent anywhere
- **Sing runs**: when the music reaches the end with Sing on, it stops and shows how the run went: the share of notes sung in time, the longest streak and the speed. A run from the voice's first note to the end, without jumping about, earns stars: one for finishing, two for 80% in time, three for 95%. The best run of each voice of each piece is kept on this device and shown as stars on the voice's chip. A run that starts later, or that you jump around in, is practice and earns none. **Again** starts over; `Space` after a result starts a new run from where the line is
- **Fill in** (a fill-in-the-blanks exercise for learning a line by heart): pick a **Voice** and some of its notes go missing. They're neither drawn nor played, and neither are notes of other voices that double them. A gold band with a question mark shows when a missing note falls, not where. The music stops there, holds the other voices and waits, and a slider under the lane wakes up with an octave of notes, the missing one somewhere among them (the octave starts somewhere new for every blank). Slide along it to hear each note against the harmony and let go on the right one; with a keyboard, `←` `→` move and `Enter` guesses. Made for a phone: sliding only listens, letting go is the guess. The keyboard and guitar necks take a guess too when they're shown. A wrong note is marked red; after three the note is given away, and `Space` gives it away at once. Where the voice has several notes at once, a blank takes the whole chord: you're asked for its notes one after another, lowest first, and each one joins the held harmony once it's found. Notes the line itself gives away are never blanked: a repeat of the note before, the middle of a scale run, a neighbour note that steps out and back, or a very short ornament. **Blanks** sets how many go missing (Few, Some, Many, All: an eighth, a quarter, half or all of the notes that qualify), and every run draws new ones at random. A run from the beginning to the end is scored by the blanks you got at the first try, with the same stars as a sung run; the best for each voice and number of blanks is kept on this device
- The **keyboard** under the lane (**Piano** under **More**) lights up every note as it sounds, in its voice's colour. Press a key to play it (in the flute's sound, or the spotlit voice's), hold several for a chord, or slide along the keys for a glissando
- Two **guitar fretboards** under the keyboard show the same notes: one in standard tuning (E A D G B E), one tuned lower for the bass line (B standard by default, or A standard, or an octave down like a Bass VI). Both keep standard tuning's shape, so chord shapes carry over. Each note goes on one string, near where that voice's hand already is. Press or slide along any fret to play it
- `Space`: play/pause · `←` `→`: jump a bar · `Home`: back to the start. The rail has Play, back to the start, **−5** and **−1** bars, **Speed**, **Hold chord**, **Sing** and **Fill in**; each mode's own controls (arpeggio, voice, guide, blanks) appear next to them only while it's on
- The **overview strip** under the lane shows the whole movement; click or drag on it to jump. Gold flags mark big tempo or metre changes (like the Ouverture's fast fugue). **Go to bar** jumps to a bar number
- Everything else is under **More**: **Sound** (Flute & strings, Random mix, or one of fourteen instruments for every voice) and **Shuffle**, volume, **reverb** amount and **space** (Room, Hall, Church), zoom, **Go to bar**, the **Piano** and **Guitar** toggles, loop and **Load MIDI…**. Whether the panel is open is remembered

## Tests

`package.json` holds development tools only; the site itself doesn't need Node.

```sh
npm install
npx playwright install chromium   # once
npm test                          # everything
npm run test:e2e                  # browser tests only (Playwright)
```

The browser tests in `tests/e2e/` drive the app the way a person would and check only what's on the page (readouts, buttons, pixels), so they keep passing however the code underneath is organised. Sing mode is tested with Chromium's fake microphone, fed with tones the tests generate (`tests/e2e/wav.js`). Tonal is served from `node_modules` during tests, so chord names don't depend on the network. GitHub Actions runs everything on every pull request.
